# Manhattan Miles: notes for Claude

First-person walking and fighting game on all of Manhattan, street by street (Mark's Vigil map: OpenStreetMap streets, buildings, parks and water, real businesses, the MTA subway): march to walk, high knees to run, steer by turning your shoulders like a steering wheel, walk to real places. Play: https://hungkach.github.io/manhattan-miles/

## Design philosophy: honest reps

Every rep counts, better reps count more, and the scoreboard doesn't lie. The full version lives in `hungkach/body-arcade` CLAUDE.md and wins over any habit here. In short:

- Effort is subjective, output is objective. A tired rep or an unfit player's best rep always works (the floor is the player's own weakest honest warm-up rep).
- Reward scales with absolute output in body units, capped at a safe range. Never re-normalize to the player's best or early-session reps: fitness gains are the progression.
- Outcomes need the reps that earn them; nothing for time spent or showing up, no XP-per-minute grind.
- Records track output across weeks (deepest squat, highest knees, fastest punch, longest bound, best cadence).
- Summaries state facts: no cheering, no praise lines, no scolding, no fatigue commentary. Rest comes from the sport's rhythm.
- Plain tone; humor aims at the world, never the player's effort.

## How this repo works

- `src/index.html` is the game. Edit it, then `npm run build` writes the playable page to `index.html` at the repo root (GitHub Pages serves that). Commit both.
- The map: `src/world.js` (`MMWorld`) decodes `data/world.b64` (gzip binary of streets, nodes, building boxes and polygons by 500 m chunk, ground polygons by 1 km tile), `data/biz.json` and `data/subway.json`, all taken from Mark's Vigil prototype artifact; the build inlines them (about 1.2 MB). Game coordinates: x east, z north, meters, origin at Times Square (the data stores z south; world.js flips it). It answers walkable, streetAt, nearestEdge, route (Dijkstra on walkable streets), intersection, buildingAt, groundAt, bizNear, stations. `test/world.test.mjs` runs it in Node.
- `engine/` is a locked copy of rep-engine from `hungkach/body-arcade`; `engine/SOURCE.json` says which commit. Never edit `engine/` here. Engine changes happen in body-arcade, then this game takes them with `node scripts/update-engine.mjs ../body-arcade` when Mark chooses.
- `npm test` must pass before every push; it also fails if `index.html` is out of date with a fresh build.
- No dependencies. Single self-contained HTML file. Plain canvas.
- `window.__game` exposes state for headless browser checks.
- Update the `BUILD` stamp in src/index.html (date and a few words) with every push; it shows on the title screen so Mark can tell when his phone is showing a cached page.

## Engine contract

- Read `engine.signals()` each frame; subscribe with `engine.on(event)`. Never touch landmarks.
- Touch demo mode (`engine.enable_demo(container)`) must keep the game fully playable; it emits the same events as the camera.
- Engine spec: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/a394f929-94ab-43c0-b06c-4d4b2cc50559

## Hard constraints

- Phone or iPad only, no accessories; living room preset, phone propped about 4 ft away, knees-up framing.
- Fitness first: the game names its S&C payoff, matches a work-to-rest rhythm, sessions 10 to 20 minutes.
- Names stay neutral and fitness oriented.

- Movement runs on `engine.locomotion()` in the free model (Mark's call after the TV test: a steering wheel, not rails). Hold a torso turn to keep turning (engine `locomotion.maxTurnRate`, 90 degrees a second at full turn), face front to go straight; marching or high knees set speed (times `T.speedScale`). Self-centering settles you onto a street's direction within 12 degrees when you stop steering. A point is a quick 90 degree turn via `set_facing`; turn around is the engine's +180. Running widens the view and says "Running".
- Fights (`Fight`, `FT` in src/index.html): the first opponent waits about 10 marching steps ahead of the start (`FT.firstAhead`), then every 75 to 110 s of walking an opponent (later sometimes two) waits ahead; reaching them calls `engine.enter_combat({ bearing, open, shooting })`. Opponent AI: guard, telegraphed straight or hook from either hand (`foe.hookSide`; wind-up glow on that hand; the full guard blocks either, and a hook is also blocked by the player's hand on its side up by the ear, `guard_left`/`guard_right`, which Mark found by accident and liked; slip dodges a straight and opens a counter), shot (sprawl stops it), step back (they come back in after 1.2 to 2 s; no marching in, it breaks the fight stance), cover-up (straights do 30% and wear the guard; hooks, uppercuts and body shots go around). Takedowns (engine 0.5.x, Mark's rework): holding the squat (`level_low`) sprawls their shot; a drive up out of it is the shot, and the engine asks `resolveShot` (the `shot` callback in `enter_combat`): braced opponents stuff anything under `FT.td.beatBrace` power (a jump beats it), and a shot timed into a wind-up, hurt, broken guard or stuffed opponent earns a longer ground and pound (`FT.td.gnpMs` 2 to 5 s, a countdown bar); on top, any fast extending punch from either hand is a down strike with no aiming (`gloveHits`, `FT.hit.gnp*`; Mark: targeting on the ground does not matter), then they scramble up. A crouch held `FT.td.braceAfterMs` makes them brace (`brace` state); an uppercut on a braced or shooting opponent does double damage ("Read it!"). Guard breaks (`FT.guard`): head straights into a cover-up wear `foe.guardHp` down; at 0 the guard breaks (`broken`, open for 1.6 s); a guard bar shows while it is worn. Every third opponent is a turtle (`foe.turtle`) who covers up a lot. Punch trade-offs: between moves they drift between close (`FT.rangeClose` 1.6 m) and mid (`FT.rangeMid` 2.3 m) range and step in to punch; straights reach mid range, hooks and uppercuts only land close (`FT.reach`, against the real distance; a whiff says "Too far for a hook"); straights hit softer, hooks hardest, uppercuts always land to the body (`FT.damage`). The HUD tag says the range. Knockout, getting dropped (10 hits) or 3 s of high knees ends it. Drawing is 3D in plain canvas: capsules and spheres projected through the game camera with near clipping and depth sorting (`capsule`, `ball`, `drawPrims`); the opponent's joints ease toward per-state poses (`targetPose`), straights fly at where your head was at the wind-up (`foe.aim`), and a ground shadow anchors them. Your arms are capsules from `engine.arms()` with a wider view-model lens (`drawArms`); a fist tilts up as it extends so a straight at shoulder height ends on their chin (`aimArms`, `armCam`). Punches land two ways, deduplicated per hand (`claim`, `FT.hit.pairMs`): the engine's `strike` events (lock-on), and the glove hitbox (`gloveHits`): a glove moving fast, out in front, that ends over their projected head or torso (`foe.hit`, set in `drawFoe`); body shots get through a cover-up. Hits show a burst, a recoil and a WebAudio thump. A level bar shows squat depth against the shot mark; a squat that is not a shot says why. The first 28 s of each camera fight is recorded (`Fight.clip`, "Save fight clip" on the summary). The camera follows your body (`povUpdate`): slip moves and rolls it, squat drops it, the shot lunges it, top looks down. Touch mode swaps to fight buttons.
